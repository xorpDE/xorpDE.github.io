Genetic distance maps from G25 coordinates

A free tool that draws a heatmap of the world or of a single region from your Global25 coordinates, entirely in your browser.

Abstract

Paste your scaled G25 coordinates, 25 numbers that summarise your autosomal DNA, and this page draws a map of the world, or of a region such as Europe or South Asia, showing which regions’ native populations are genetically closest to you, together with a table of the closest reference populations. The map is computed locally in your browser: nothing is uploaded, no account is needed, and the tool is free.

2How it works

Everything happens on your own device, in four steps:

  1. Compare. Your 25 coordinates are compared with those of about 2,000 reference populations. Each population gets a genetic distance: the lower the number, the more similar.
  2. Place. Every population sits at its homeland, where it traditionally lives. Diaspora and outlier groups are left out, so the map shows regions of origin rather than where people live today.
  3. Fill in. The space between the populations is filled in smoothly, so every place on the map gets a value.
  4. Colour. Only land is coloured: red where people are closest to you, dark blue at or beyond the maximum distance you chose. Thin lines mark steps of 0.01.

The maps use the same projection as most online maps (Web Mercator). “Save this view” draws the part you zoomed into as a map of its own.

4Questions

What are G25 coordinates?

Global25 (G25) is a genetic coordinate system from the Eurogenes project by Davidski. It summarises a person’s autosomal DNA as 25 numbers: a point in a 25-dimensional space. The closer two points are, the more similar the people or populations behind them.

How do I get my coordinates?

You need the raw data file from a DNA test (for example AncestryDNA, 23andMe, MyHeritage or FamilyTreeDNA) and an independent service that converts it into G25 coordinates, such as Davidski’s g25requests.app. This site is not affiliated with any of them and never needs your raw data, only the line with the 25 numbers.

Scaled or unscaled coordinates?

Use the scaled coordinates. G25 services usually provide both; the reference populations, the distances and the colour scale here are all based on scaled values, so unscaled numbers give a misleading map.

Is anything uploaded?

No. Your browser downloads the map engine (Python with Pyodide, from the jsDelivr CDN) and the reference data from this site. Your coordinates are then processed only on your device and are never sent anywhere. There are no accounts, cookies or analytics; the only thing stored is your light or dark choice, in your own browser.

Why is the first map slower?

The first time, your browser downloads and starts the map engine: Python with NumPy, SciPy and matplotlib, compiled to WebAssembly. This takes about 10–30 seconds, and the browser keeps a copy for later visits. Each map then takes some seconds of computing on your own device; phones are slower than laptops. A world map takes longer, about 30–90 seconds.

What do the colours and the numbers mean?

Every point on land is coloured by the estimated G25 distance between your coordinates and the people native to that place. Red is closest; the colours run through orange, yellow and green to blue, and dark blue means at or beyond the maximum distance (0.08 by default). Contour lines mark every 0.01. As a rough guide, a distance below about 0.02 is a very close match, around 0.05 is typical between populations from different parts of Europe, and 0.10 or more is far apart. Can I use the image?

Yes. The map is yours to download, share or print. The basemap is in the public domain (Natural Earth), and the data sources are listed in section 5.
